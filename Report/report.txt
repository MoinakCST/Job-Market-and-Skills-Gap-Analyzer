Producers send messages to a queue, and consumers process them independently. This enhances scalability by allowing services to process tasks at their own pace, improves fault tolerance by retrying failed messages, and decouples services, preventing direct dependencies. Kafka is a distributed streaming platform, excellent for high-throughput, sequential, and replayable data streams, often used for log aggregation or real-time analytics. RabbitMQ is a message broker, better suited for routing and complex message patterns, often used for task queues and guaranteed message delivery for individual tasks. I'd choose Kafka for stream processing where message order and replayability are key, and RabbitMQ for traditional task queuing and complex routing scenarios within a microservices setup."

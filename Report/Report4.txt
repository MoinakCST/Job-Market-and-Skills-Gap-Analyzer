                "answer": "Message queues act as an intermediary for asynchronous communication between different parts of an application or microservices. They allow producers to send messages without waiting for consumers to process them immediately, decoupling services and improving system resilience. Kafka and RabbitMQ both serve this purpose but differ in their strengths; Kafka is generally geared for high-throughput stream processing, while RabbitMQ is often chosen for reliable message delivery and complex routing. In an e-commerce context, a message queue would be invaluable for handling order processing. For example, when a customer places an order, instead of directly calling multiple downstream services (payment, inventory, shipping, email notification), the order service can publish an 'Order Placed' message to a queue. Separate services can then asynchronously consume this message to process payment, update inventory, initiate shipping, and send an email notification. This makes the system more scalable (as services can process at their own pace), resilient (if one service fails, the message remains in the queue for later processing), and improves user experience by quickly confirming the order without waiting for all backend processes to complete."
